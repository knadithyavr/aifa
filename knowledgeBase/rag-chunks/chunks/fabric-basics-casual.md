# Fabric Basics for Casual Wear

Cotton vs linen vs polyester vs blends -- what to look for in t-shirts, jeans, chinos, and casual shirts. GSM explained.

---

## Why Fabric Matters

Two t-shirts can look identical on a screen but feel completely different in person. The difference is fabric. Fabric determines how a garment breathes, drapes, holds its shape, ages, and feels against your skin. For Indian men dealing with heat, humidity, and long commutes, fabric choice is arguably more important than style choice.

---

## The Big Four Fabrics for Casual Wear

### Cotton

**What it is:** Natural plant fibre. The most common fabric in menswear.

**Strengths:** Breathable, absorbent, comfortable, versatile, easy to care for. Gets softer with every wash.

**Weaknesses:** Wrinkles easily (especially lightweight weaves), absorbs and holds moisture (slow to dry), can shrink if not pre-washed.

**Best for:** T-shirts, polos, chinos, casual shirts, underwear. Cotton is the foundation of any Indian man's wardrobe.

**Quality indicators:**
- Combed cotton: Short fibres removed, leaving longer, stronger strands. Softer and more uniform.
- Ringspun cotton: Spun more tightly for a softer, stronger yarn.
- Supima/Pima cotton: Extra-long staple cotton. Superior softness and durability. Worth the premium.
- Organic cotton: Grown without synthetic pesticides. Same properties as conventional cotton, but more sustainable.

**Indian context:** Cotton is ideal for 8-9 months of the Indian year. For peak summer, choose lightweight weaves. For monsoon, choose tighter weaves or cotton-polyester blends that dry faster.

### Linen

**What it is:** Natural fibre from the flax plant. The strongest natural textile fibre.

**Strengths:** Extremely breathable, cool to touch, moisture-wicking, dries faster than cotton. Natural luster. Biodegradable. Gets softer with age.

**Weaknesses:** Wrinkles heavily (the number one complaint). Less structured than cotton. Can feel stiff when new. Some versions shrink when washed.

**Best for:** Summer shirts, casual trousers, summer blazers. The quintessential Indian summer fabric.

**The wrinkle question:** Linen wrinkles are a feature, not a bug. Light rumpling is expected and accepted -- it signals "I chose a premium natural fabric." Heavy, crumpled-paper wrinkling suggests the garment does not fit or has not been cared for. The solution: linen blends.

**Indian context:** Linen is the MVP of Indian summer (March-June). It is expensive but worth it. For monsoon, use with caution -- it can shrink when wet and looks disheveled after heavy rain.

### Polyester

**What it is:** Synthetic fibre made from petroleum-based polymers.

**Strengths:** Wrinkle-resistant, durable, holds shape well, dries very fast, does not shrink, relatively inexpensive.

**Weaknesses:** Does not breathe well. Traps heat and odour. Can feel plasticky against skin. Not sustainable. Prone to pilling over time.

**Best for:** Blending with natural fibres (cotton-poly blends) to add durability and quick-dry properties. Also: athletic wear, rain jackets, and monsoon-specific clothing.

**Indian context:** 100% polyester next to the skin is miserable in Indian heat. But polyester blends (65% cotton / 35% polyester) are practical for monsoon season and travel. The polyester adds quick-dry properties without completely sacrificing breathability.

### Denim (Cotton Twill)

**What it is:** A durable cotton twill weave, traditionally using indigo-dyed warp and white weft threads.

**Strengths:** Extremely durable, ages beautifully, versatile, holds its shape well.

**Weaknesses:** Heavy, slow to dry, can be hot in summer, heavy denim is restrictive.

**Best for:** Jeans (obviously), denim jackets, some casual shirts (lighter weight).

**Quality indicators:**
- Weight: Measured in ounces per square yard. 10-12 oz is standard. 14+ oz is heavy raw denim. Under 10 oz is lightweight summer denim.
- Stretch: 1-3% elastane adds comfort. Pure cotton denim is stiffer but fades better.
- Selvedge: Woven on traditional shuttle looms creating a self-finished edge. A quality marker, but not a guarantee of superiority.

## Fabric Blends: The Best of Both Worlds

### Cotton-Linen Blend
Combines linen's breathability with cotton's softness and structure. Reduces wrinkling by 40-50% compared to pure linen. The best office-appropriate summer fabric for India.

### Cotton-Polyester Blend
Adds durability, wrinkle resistance, and quick-dry properties to cotton. The practical choice for monsoon and travel. A 65/35 cotton-poly blend is a good balance.

### Cotton-Elastane (Stretch)
1-3% elastane/lycra added to cotton for stretch and recovery. Essential for slim-fit jeans and fitted chinos. Makes movement comfortable without the fabric going baggy.

### Tri-Blend (Cotton-Polyester-Rayon)
Ultra-soft, lightweight, excellent drape. Common in premium t-shirts. The rayon adds a silk-like feel. Comfortable but less durable than pure cotton.

## GSM Explained: The Number You Should Check

GSM stands for **Grams per Square Metre.** It measures fabric weight -- how thick and heavy a piece of fabric is per unit area. For t-shirts and casual knits, GSM is the most useful quality indicator you can check before buying.

### T-Shirt GSM Guide

| GSM | Feel | Best For | Price Indicator |
|-----|------|----------|-----------------|
| 120-150 | Lightweight, thin, airy | Indian summer, layering, gym | Budget basics |
| 150-180 | Medium, balanced, everyday | Year-round daily wear | Mid-range |
| 180-200 | Substantial, structured | Cooler months, premium feel | Premium basics |
| 220+ | Thick, rigid, luxe | Winter, streetwear | Designer/premium streetwear |

**The sweet spot for India:** 150-180 GSM for everyday tees. Light enough for summer but thick enough to not be see-through.

**The opacity rule:** White t-shirts below 150 GSM are often translucent. If you want an opaque white tee, go for 160 GSM minimum.

### Polo Shirt Fabric Guide

Polos use different fabric types than t-shirts:

- **Pique cotton (220-260 GSM):** The classic polo fabric. Waffle/honeycomb texture provides superior airflow. The best choice for Indian heat.
- **Jersey cotton (150-200 GSM):** Smoother, more casual. Softer but less structured. Can drape and look less crisp than pique.
- **Performance blend:** Moisture-wicking synthetic-natural blends. Great for athletics and monsoon.

## Fabric by Garment: What to Look For

### T-Shirts
- **Ideal fabric:** 100% combed/ringspun cotton, 150-180 GSM
- **Premium upgrade:** Supima cotton, bamboo blend, or tri-blend
- **Summer:** 120-150 GSM cotton or bamboo
- **Winter:** 180-220 GSM cotton or French terry
- **Check:** Does it feel soft? Is it opaque when stretched? Does the collar bounce back when tugged?

### Jeans
- **Ideal fabric:** Cotton denim with 1-2% elastane, 10-12 oz weight
- **Raw/selvedge:** 100% cotton, 12-14 oz (for enthusiasts willing to break in)
- **Summer:** Lightweight denim under 10 oz or cotton-linen denim
- **Check:** Does the denim feel dense and substantial? Is the colour even? Does it have slight stretch when pulled?

### Chinos
- **Ideal fabric:** Cotton twill, 200-280 GSM
- **Summer:** Cotton-linen blend or lightweight cotton twill
- **With stretch:** Cotton twill with 2-3% elastane for comfort
- **Check:** Does it drape smoothly without wrinkling excessively? Does it hold a crease? Is the fabric dense enough to not show underwear lines?

### Casual Shirts
- **Oxford cloth:** Basket-weave cotton. Durable, slightly textured. The OCBD staple.
- **Linen:** For summer. Accept some wrinkling.
- **Chambray:** Lightweight plain-weave. Looks like light denim but softer and more breathable.
- **Poplin:** Tightly woven plain-weave. Smooth, light, crisp. Good summer option.
- **Flannel:** Brushed cotton. Warm, soft. For winter.
- **Check:** Does the collar stand up without a tie? Is the fabric thick enough to not be see-through? Does it drape naturally without looking stiff?

## Red Flags When Buying

1. **No fabric composition listed.** If a brand does not tell you what the garment is made of, it is likely cheap polyester.
2. **"Cotton feel" or "cotton touch."** These are marketing terms for polyester that feels like cotton. Check the actual composition.
3. **Very low price + claims of premium fabric.** 100% Supima cotton at Rs 300 is not real. Quality natural fibres cost money.
4. **Pilling after first wash.** This indicates low-quality fibres or poor construction. Buy from brands with return policies so you can test before committing.
5. **Excessive shrinkage.** Quality garments are pre-shrunk. If a t-shirt shrinks a full size after one wash, the fabric is not pre-treated.
