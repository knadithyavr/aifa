# What to Wear to an Indian Wedding (Guest Guide)

Function-by-function breakdown: haldi, mehendi, sangeet, pheras, and reception -- with colour rules and traditional vs Western options.

---

## The Big Idea

Indian weddings are not one event -- they are five or more events over multiple days. Each function has a different vibe, formality level, and dress code. Wearing the same outfit to haldi and reception is like wearing gym clothes to a job interview.

The good news: you do not need a sherwani for every function. Strategic use of kurtas, Nehru jackets, and a single blazer can carry you through the entire wedding week.

---

## Haldi Ceremony

**Vibe:** Daytime, outdoors, messy. Turmeric is being smeared on people. Think festive but disposable.

**Formality:** The lowest of all wedding functions. Comfort and colour first.

**What to wear:**
- **Lightweight cotton kurta** in a bright colour -- yellow (traditional haldi colour), mustard, orange, or warm bright tones
- **Cotton pyjama or churidar** bottoms
- **Kolhapuri chappals or sandals** -- your shoes will get stained
- Skip heavy jewellery entirely

**Colour rules:**
- Bright yellows, deep mustard, warm orange, and sunny tones are expected and appropriate
- White is technically fine but will stain permanently from turmeric
- Avoid dark formal colours -- this is a joyful, colourful event

**Western option:** A bright-coloured linen shirt + white or light chinos + sandals. Works if you are not in the inner circle.

**Practical tip:** Wear something you do not mind getting stained. Turmeric does not wash out easily.

---

## Mehendi Ceremony

**Vibe:** Relaxed, festive daytime event. Henna being applied. Music and snacks.

**Formality:** Low to moderate. Casual yet festive.

**What to wear:**
- **Short kurta with Nehru jacket** -- the go-to combination
- **Printed cotton kurta** in festive patterns
- **Casual dhoti-style bottoms** or churidar
- **Mojris or Kolhapuri sandals** for footwear

**Colour rules:**
- Shades of green are traditional for mehendi (represents new beginnings)
- Also great: rust, mustard, turquoise, pink, deep coral
- Printed and patterned fabrics are welcome -- this is the function where you can experiment

**Western option:** A printed camp-collar shirt or a linen shirt in a bold colour + chinos + loafers. Add a Nehru jacket over a Western shirt for a fusion look.

**Tip:** Mehendi is where Indo-Western fusion works best. Kurta with jeans, Nehru jacket over a polo -- mix it up.

---

## Sangeet

**Vibe:** High energy. Evening event with dance performances, music, and celebration. The party function.

**Formality:** Medium-high. Glamorous and bold.

**What to wear:**
- **Kurta with pyjama or dhoti** in rich fabrics -- silk, art silk, brocade
- **Jodhpuri suit** for a sharper look
- **Rich jewel tones and metallics** -- this is the time to go bold
- **Jodhpuri mojaris** or embroidered ethnic shoes
- Statement accessories are welcome -- brooch, layered necklace, pocket square

**Colour rules:**
- Jewel tones: emerald, sapphire blue, deep purple, wine red
- Metallics: gold accents, champagne, silver
- Bold is good. This is a dance party, not a boardroom.

**Western option:** A dark blazer (navy or black) + dress shirt (bold colour or pattern) + dress trousers + dress shoes. Add a pocket square.

**Fabric tip:** Choose fabrics with slight sheen (silk, art silk) that catch light. The sangeet is usually well-lit and photographed. Matte cotton looks flat in evening light.

**Movement tip:** You may be dancing. Ensure your outfit allows freedom of movement. A too-tight sherwani on the dance floor is misery.

---

## Pheras (Main Wedding Ceremony)

**Vibe:** Sacred, traditional, formal. The most important function.

**Formality:** Highest for traditional wear. Regal and respectful.

**What to wear:**
- **Sherwani** for the most formal look -- the gold standard for male wedding guests at pheras
- **Bandhgala suit** for a sophisticated alternative
- **Classic kurta set** in premium fabric (silk, velvet, brocade) for a simpler but still appropriate option
- **Mojaris or juttis** for footwear
- Minimal but quality accessories

**Colour rules (critical):**
- **AVOID RED** -- red is reserved for the bride. Wearing red to a wedding is a serious faux pas.
- **AVOID WHITE** -- associated with mourning in Indian culture. Off-white/cream is acceptable.
- **AVOID BLACK** -- considered inauspicious at many Indian weddings, especially by older family members.
- **Best colours:** Royal blue, emerald green, deep pink/magenta, maroon/wine, champagne, gold accents, ivory (not pure white)

**Western option:** Not ideal for pheras if you are close family or from the same cultural background. A dark suit (not black) with a festive pocket square can work if you are a distant guest or from a different cultural background.

**Tip:** When in doubt about formality, a well-fitted bandhgala in a deep colour is the safest choice. It signals effort and respect without the full commitment of a sherwani.

---

## Reception

**Vibe:** The red-carpet function. Evening glamour. Cocktails, photos, and celebration.

**Formality:** Highest overall. The most Western-influenced function.

**What to wear -- Option A (Western formal):**
- **Tuxedo** with satin accents (if you own one)
- **Dark suit** in navy, charcoal, or midnight blue (more accessible)
- **Velvet blazer** in navy, burgundy, or forest green + dress trousers (the statement option)
- **Double-breasted jacket** for extra impact
- **Oxford shoes** or monk straps
- Bow tie or a silk tie, metallic lapel pin, pocket square

**What to wear -- Option B (Indo-Western / Ethnic formal):**
- **Premium bandhgala suit** in a rich fabric
- **Heavily embroidered sherwani** (different from what you wore to pheras)
- **Jodhpuri suit** in a dark shade with contrast detailing

**Colour rules:**
- Darker, richer tones: midnight blue, charcoal, deep burgundy, forest green
- Metallics and sheen are appropriate -- velvet, silk, satin accents
- The reception is where you dress your absolute best

**Tip:** The reception is the most photographed function. Invest in looking good here if you have to pick one function to splurge on.

## Budget Strategy: Covering All Functions

You do not need five different outfits. Here is how to stretch your wardrobe:

1. **One cotton kurta + pyjama set** (bright colour): Covers haldi and mehendi
2. **One premium kurta or Nehru jacket**: Covers mehendi (dressed up) and sangeet (paired with different bottoms)
3. **One sherwani or bandhgala**: Covers pheras and (with different styling) reception
4. **One dark blazer** (navy or black): Reception backup, also works for sangeet if paired with festive shirt

**Total: 3-4 outfits for 5 functions.**

## General Wedding Guest Tips

- **Check the invite or ask.** Some families specify colour themes for each function.
- **Comfort matters.** Indian weddings are long. If your sherwani chokes you, you will not enjoy yourself.
- **Footwear will come off.** At many Indian venues, you remove shoes at the door. Ensure your socks are clean and in good condition.
- **Pocket basics:** Phone, wallet, and handkerchief. Leave everything else behind.
- **Grooming > clothing.** A well-groomed man in a simple kurta looks better than an unkempt man in an expensive sherwani.
