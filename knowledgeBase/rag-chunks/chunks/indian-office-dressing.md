# Indian Office Dressing: Tech Casual to Corporate

What 80% of Indian office workers actually wear, and how to nail it without overthinking.

---

## The Two Office Realities in India

Indian office dress codes split into two clear worlds, and most advice online is written for the Western corporate world, which does not apply here.

### Tech / Startup Offices (The Majority)
90% of Indian startups and tech companies have ditched formal wear. The dress code is unwritten but real: look clean, look intentional, do not look like you just rolled out of bed.

### Corporate / Traditional Offices
Banking, consulting, large Indian conglomerates (Tata, Reliance, Mahindra), law firms. These still expect a degree of formality -- blazers, dress shirts, polished shoes.

## Tech Office Dressing

### What Actually Works

**The daily uniform most tech workers default to:**
- Clean tee (crew or polo) + chinos or dark jeans + clean sneakers

**That is the 80% formula.** Here is how to do it well:

**Top options (pick one):**
- Plain crew neck tee in white, navy, grey, or olive (no graphics, no slogans for a clean look)
- Polo shirt in a solid colour (the instant upgrade)
- Henley for variety
- OCBD (Oxford cloth button-down) with sleeves rolled for the "I care but not too much" look

**Bottom options (pick one):**
- Dark indigo jeans (the workhorse)
- Chinos in navy, khaki, olive, or charcoal
- Chino joggers (if your office is truly casual)

**Shoe options:**
- White minimalist sneakers (the default)
- Clean court sneakers (Stan Smiths, Puma CA Pro)
- Loafers if you want to lean smart-casual

**What to avoid:**
- Flip-flops and chappals (even if others wear them -- you are reading this to look better than average)
- Gym shorts and tank tops
- Distressed or ripped jeans (subtle whiskering is fine)
- Graphic tees with offensive or juvenile prints
- Clothes that are visibly wrinkled or stained

### Levelling Up in Tech

When you have a client meeting, presentation, or team dinner, upgrade from the daily formula:

- Swap the tee for an OCBD or a linen shirt
- Swap sneakers for loafers or desert boots
- Add a lightweight bomber or unstructured blazer
- Swap jeans for chinos if you were in denim

This takes you from "tech casual" to "smart casual" in two garment swaps.

## Corporate Office Dressing

### The Indian Corporate Uniform

**Daily standard:**
- Crisp dress shirt (white, light blue, or subtle pattern) + well-fitting dress trousers (charcoal, navy, or medium grey) + leather shoes (black or dark brown)

**Upgraded standard:**
- Add a blazer or sport coat for meetings, presentations, and client interactions
- Nehru jacket or bandhgala for semi-formal corporate events -- uniquely Indian and sharp

**Shirt rules:**
- White and light blue are the foundation. You need 3-4 of each.
- Subtle patterns (micro-checks, fine stripes) add variety without being flashy.
- Long sleeves always, even in summer. Roll them neatly above the elbow if your office culture allows it.
- Well-pressed. An un-ironed shirt in a corporate office signals carelessness.

**Trouser rules:**
- Flat-front, mid-rise, tapered or straight cut.
- Charcoal and navy are the most versatile. Add medium grey and a pair of khaki/beige chinos for variety.
- Pressed with a front crease. Wrinkled trousers are a visible mistake in corporate settings.

**Shoe rules:**
- Black leather Oxfords or Derbys for formal. Brown leather brogues or loafers for business-casual days.
- Always polished. Scuffed shoes undermine an otherwise good outfit.
- Belt colour must match shoe colour. Always.

## Summer Office Hacks (March-June)

Indian summers are brutal, and the commute from a 45-degree outdoor to an 18-degree AC office creates a unique challenge. Here is how to handle it.

### The Fabric Solution
- **Cotton-linen blends** are the MVP. They breathe outdoors and look polished indoors.
- **Pure linen shirts** are excellent for heat but wrinkle fast. Acceptable in smart-casual offices. In corporate settings, go for linen blends.
- **Poplin and lightweight cotton twill** for shirts -- smooth, breathable, maintains shape.
- **Avoid polyester** next to the skin. It traps heat and causes sweat patches.

### The Layering Solution (AC to Outdoor)
The key strategy: start with a breathable base layer and add a light mid-layer for the cold AC.

- **Base:** Cotton or linen shirt (handles outdoor heat)
- **Mid-layer for AC:** A lightweight unstructured blazer, cotton cardigan, or packable gilet
- **Shed the mid-layer** when stepping outside

This way you are comfortable at your desk and not soaking through your shirt on the commute.

### Light Colour Strategy
- Light colours (white, cream, sky blue, soft pink, pale grey) reflect sunlight and reduce heat absorption during the commute.
- Save your dark navy and charcoal for monsoon and winter months when heat management is less critical.

### Practical Summer Tips
- **Carry a spare shirt** if your commute involves public transport. Change at the office.
- **Undershirts** in cotton or bamboo fabric absorb sweat and prevent visible sweat patches on your outer shirt. The V-neck undershirt hides beneath an open collar.
- **Relaxed fits** with room between skin and fabric allow airflow. Cuban collars and band collars offer extra ventilation.
- **Lightweight loafers or breathable leather shoes** instead of heavy leather boots.
- **Minimise accessories** -- heavy watches, layered chains, and thick belts add heat.

## Monsoon Office Survival

- **Dark colours top and bottom** -- hide splash marks and water stains.
- **Quick-dry fabrics** or cotton-polyester blends that do not stay damp.
- **Waterproof bag** -- protect your laptop and documents.
- **Spare shoes at the office** -- commute in waterproof footwear, change into leather shoes at your desk.
- **Skip suede and canvas** shoes entirely during monsoon.
- **A packable rain jacket** in your bag -- lightweight, breathable, foldable.

## The Unwritten Rules

1. **Match your boss's level or one step above.** If your boss wears polos, wear polos. If they occasionally wear a blazer, keep one at your desk.
2. **The first week at a new job, overdress slightly.** Observe what others wear, then calibrate. It is easier to dress down than to recover from looking sloppy on day one.
3. **Grooming counts as much as clothing.** Clean nails, trimmed beard, clean shoes, no wrinkled clothes. These basics matter more than brand names.
4. **Your laptop bag is part of your outfit.** A ratty backpack with a nice outfit is a mismatch. A clean, minimal backpack or messenger bag completes the look.
