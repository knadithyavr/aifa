# Collar Types, Necklines, and Sunglasses for Your Face Shape

How to choose necklines, collars, and eyewear that frame your face in the most flattering way.

---

## The Frame Principle

Think of your face as a picture and everything around it -- collar, neckline, glasses -- as the frame. A small picture needs a proportionate frame. A wide picture needs a wider frame. When the frame matches the picture, everything looks balanced and your face becomes the focal point.

This is not about fashion trends. It is about your physical proportions.

---

## Identifying Your Face Shape

Stand in front of a mirror. Pull your hair back if it covers your forehead. Look at the overall outline of your face.

**Round face:** Width and length are roughly equal. Soft jawline, full cheeks. Your face is widest at the cheekbones.

**Oval face:** Slightly longer than wide. Forehead slightly wider than jaw. Gently rounded jawline. Considered the most versatile shape.

**Square face:** Strong, angular jawline. Forehead, cheekbones, and jaw are roughly the same width. Prominent jaw corners.

**Oblong/long face:** Noticeably longer than wide. Forehead, cheeks, and jawline are similar width. Can have a high forehead or long chin.

**Wide/broad face:** Wider than average, often with prominent cheekbones. Can be round-wide or square-wide.

**Narrow/thin face:** Longer and narrower than average. Small delicate features. Forehead and jaw are narrow.

## Collars and Necklines by Face Shape

### Round Face

**Goal:** Add vertical length. Break the circular outline. Slim down the perceived width.

**Best collars:**
- **V-neck t-shirts:** The V creates a vertical line that elongates your face. A shallow to medium V is ideal.
- **Point collars** on casual shirts: The downward-pointing collar tips draw the eye vertically, counteracting roundness.
- **Button-down collars** with a natural roll: The soft points narrow the visual frame.
- **Henley necklines:** The buttoned placket adds a vertical detail at the centre.

**Avoid:**
- Crew necks that echo your round face shape.
- Spread collars that add width.
- Boat necks that run horizontally across the shoulders.

### Oval Face

**Goal:** Maintain balance. You can wear almost anything.

**Best collars:**
- **Crew neck:** Classic, clean. Your face shape handles the round neckline without looking wider.
- **V-neck:** Works well at any depth.
- **Spread collar and point collar:** Both work. You have the most freedom.
- **Button-down collar:** A natural fit for casual settings.

**Note:** Oval faces are the most versatile. Focus on proportion -- match collar size to your overall head and body size rather than worrying about face shape.

### Square / Angular Face

**Goal:** Soften the angular jawline. Add some curve to balance the straight lines.

**Best collars:**
- **Crew neck:** The soft, round neckline contrasts with your angular jaw.
- **Spread collar** with moderate width: The wider angle softens the square jaw.
- **Club/round collar** on casual shirts: Rounded collar tips echo nothing angular.
- **Scoop neck:** The wider U-shape adds softness.

**Avoid:**
- Sharp, narrow point collars that echo and emphasize your angular features.
- Mandarin/band collars that create another hard horizontal line near your jaw.

### Oblong / Long Face

**Goal:** Add width. Break the vertical length. Create horizontal emphasis.

**Best collars:**
- **Crew neck and boat neck:** Horizontal necklines that shorten the face.
- **Spread collar** (wide): Wide collar points add horizontal emphasis and visually widen.
- **Camp collar / Cuban collar:** The open, flat collar creates a horizontal V that breaks the long face.

**Avoid:**
- Deep V-necks that add more vertical length.
- Long, narrow point collars that elongate further.
- Turtlenecks and mock necks that add height above the collar.

### Wide / Broad Face

**Goal:** Narrow the perceived width. Create vertical emphasis.

**Best collars:**
- **V-neck** (medium to deep): The V creates a strong vertical line.
- **Point collar** (longer points): Draws the eye downward and narrows.
- **Henley:** The vertical placket slims the frame.
- **Mandarin/band collar:** The vertical band collar adds height without width.

**Avoid:**
- Wide spread collars that match your width.
- Boat necks that emphasize horizontal breadth.
- Crew necks on their own (they do not narrow).

### Narrow / Thin Face

**Goal:** Add width. Make the face appear slightly fuller.

**Best collars:**
- **Crew neck:** Adds horizontal balance at the neckline.
- **Spread collar** with soft construction: Adds width near the face.
- **Boat neck:** Horizontal emphasis that fills out the frame.

**Avoid:**
- Deep V-necks and long point collars that make a thin face look even narrower and longer.
- Turtlenecks that compress the frame.

## Sunglasses by Face Shape

Sunglasses follow one core rule: **contrast your face shape.** Round face benefits from angular frames. Angular face benefits from softer, rounder frames.

### Round Face
- **Best:** Rectangular, square, or angular frames (Wayfarers, square aviators). The straight lines add definition.
- **Avoid:** Round frames that echo your face shape.

### Oval Face
- **Best:** Almost any shape works. Wayfarers, aviators, round frames, rectangular -- you have the most freedom.
- **Keep in mind:** The frame should be as wide as (or slightly wider than) the broadest part of your face.

### Square Face
- **Best:** Round frames, oval frames, and aviators. Curves soften angular features.
- **Also good:** Browline frames (clubmasters) where the top is heavier.
- **Avoid:** Small, narrow rectangular frames that emphasize your angles.

### Oblong / Long Face
- **Best:** Oversized frames, aviators, round frames, wayfarers. Large frames fill the vertical space and shorten the appearance.
- **Avoid:** Small, narrow frames that get lost on a long face.

### Wide / Broad Face
- **Best:** Frames that are at least as wide as your face. Rectangular or slightly angular shapes. Angular frames add structure without adding width.
- **Avoid:** Narrow frames that make your face look even wider by contrast.

### Narrow / Thin Face
- **Best:** Round frames, oversized frames, and wide aviators that add visual width.
- **Avoid:** Narrow, angular frames that echo the narrowness.

## Practical Tips for Indian Men

- **If you are unsure of your face shape,** start with a button-down collar for casual shirts and a crew neck for t-shirts. These are the safest starting points.
- **Most Indian men have round to oval face shapes.** V-necks and point collars tend to be universally flattering.
- **When buying sunglasses in India,** brands like John Jacobs, Lenskart, and Vincent Chase offer affordable options. Try on multiple shapes -- the "try at home" programs let you test before buying.
- **Collar size should match body scale too.** If you are a big guy with a broad face, a tiny collar looks lost. If you have a small frame and delicate features, an oversized collar overwhelms. The collar frames the face -- it should be proportional to what it frames.
